# Medieval
Also: castle, castles, knights, keep, fortress, village medieval

Castles, walls, towers, and the timber-and-stone villages around them, at figure scale unless the build settings say
otherwise.

## Rules

- Stone in two grays, never one: light and dark bluish gray mixed course by course, with a little tan or dark tan for
  repairs and strata (`thick_wall`, `masonry_wall`, `irregular_stone_wall`, `white_ashlar_wall` for a pale
  fortress). One gray reads as concrete.
- Walls are thick and tall: 2 studs or more, with a wall walk on top behind `battlements` or `fin_merlon_crenels`,
  `machicolation` or `wooden_hoarding` over the gate, `buttress`es on long runs.
- Towers break the line every 12 to 16 studs: square, `round_tower`, `round_bastion`, `bartizan` on a corner, capped
  with a `tapered_tower_cap` or `pyramid_spire_tower`.
- Openings are small and few: `arrow_slit`s, a gate with a `portcullis` and a `drawbridge`, arches
  (`brick_built_arch`, `double_arch_colonnade`).
- The village is timber over stone: a stone ground floor, a `jettied_floor` above in `half_timber_band` or
  `timber_frame_panels`, steep dark roofs, a `chimney_stack`.
- Color the life in with few accents: `wall_banner`s, `torch_sconce`s, a red or blue shield, a market stall's awning.
- A castle is a playset: open at the back, with a great hall (a long table, a throne, a `hearth_fireplace`), a
  dungeon, and stairs to the wall walk (`guide interiors`).
- The ground rises to the walls: a rocky hill or a moat (`hillside`, `rock_outcrop`, `cliff_wall`, `river`).

## Scale

At figure scale a gate is about 5 bricks tall and battlements are 1 x 2 merlons a brick tall with 1-stud gaps. To make
a castle or city read as huge, keep what figures touch at figure size and put micro houses, windows, and towers on the
walls and tiers above it (`guide micro`).
